Water is the constraint that decides whether a smallholder vegetable farm makes money. Not land, not labour — water, and the cost of moving it.

On our Machakos plots we cut water use by roughly 40 per cent without reducing yield. Almost none of that came from buying a better pump.

## Start with scheduling, not hardware

The cheapest water saving is not irrigating when you do not need to. Two habits do most of the work:

1. **Water early morning or late evening.** Midday irrigation loses a large share to evaporation before it reaches the root zone.
2. **Irrigate to root depth, not to the surface.** A short daily sprinkle trains roots upward and makes plants more drought-fragile, not less.

A tensiometer — or simply a hand pushed into the soil at root depth — will tell you more than a timer will.

## Drip on a smallholder budget

The commercial drip kits are priced for export operations. Two cheaper routes work:

### Gravity-fed drip from a raised tank

A tank on a two-metre stand gives enough head pressure for drip tape on a plot of up to about half an acre, with no pump and no electricity. The tape itself is the main cost, and it lasts three to five seasons with care.

### Bottle and bucket micro-irrigation

For kitchen gardens and nursery beds, an inverted bottle or a bucket with a pinhole delivers water slowly to the root zone for cents. Unglamorous, and very effective.

## What we actually installed

| Measure | Cost band | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gravity drip from raised tanks | Low | ~35% less water than furrow |
| Mulch on all beds | Very low | Large cut in evaporation; also suppresses weeds |
| Drip tape in place of furrow | Medium | Better uniformity, less disease from wet foliage |
| Simple shade net on nursery beds | Low | Higher seedling survival in hot months |

Note what is *not* on that list: a borehole upgrade. Our water source was adequate. The problem was distribution, and distribution is cheap to fix.

## The harder part — soil

Water retention is a soil property. Every season of compost or manure added to these plots raises organic matter, and every point of organic matter raises the water the soil will hold. Over three seasons this effect was worth more than the drip tape.

## What it returned

- Water use down roughly 40 per cent against our furrow baseline.
- Yield flat to slightly up — the uniformity of drip matters more than the total volume applied.
- Labour down, because moving pipes is most of the labour in furrow irrigation.
